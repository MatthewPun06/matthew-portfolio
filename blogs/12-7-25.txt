Recently, I’ve been stacking rocks. Not quite literally, but it may as well be. I’ve been building towers for months, but now I’ve been slowly taking off rocks one at a time–differential equations, data structures and algorithms, systems programming–and it’s left me with nothing to do. I’ve been so used to seeing that tall tower, it’s difficult to go back to having only a short and stable base without needing to stack something new on top.  So, I did what people normally do: go for 14 mile long runs and doom scroll on the floor because I can’t walk. What is it about seeing that gap in my stack and wanting to fill it? 
After knocking a part of it over, it’s nice to leave it empty and really think about which rocks to stack next. If you rush to add more, you could distract yourself with stones that don’t really interest you. When you take the time to search for something new, you might be able to stack unique rocks, gems, and crystals to create new art. If you take too much time, nothing will get built and you’re left on the ground unable to see the view from the sky. 
Starting over is a part of the process. It’s what makes rock stacking beautiful. It’s also what makes autumn, flowers, and sunrises so beloved. When you build up something for the first time, it’s never perfect. There is always something you’re missing or something that you could do better, so why settle for anything less when it’s your life? You can be content and want more (call me greedy if you want). 
So with this empty space, I’ll use it to look around for something new to do. I might pick up a couple old rocks to see how they play with new ones. I might read, start a show, compose, crochet, decorate, or stack real rocks. But, rest assured, empty space is never a void. It just means it’s time to explore.
