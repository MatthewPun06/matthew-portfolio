There are certain moments during the day that seem to stop time. For many of us, that peace comes late at night when the world falls asleep. The noise will fade away, allowing us to hear our own thoughts.
The leaves stop rustling, the cars and buses freeze, and people stand still in the early morning for me; the world falls silent as I walk around campus; and the sky is still dark as the snow falls like feathers sweeping to the ground. This past week, mornings have been magical as the branches carry layers of snow and the ground becomes a tundra of shimmering white.
A recent goal I have had is to start off my days intentionally. I’ll wake up before the sun rises, enjoying the ability to watch the sky go from navy blue to a cozy orange and then a brilliant blue. Through the silence and stasis, I’ll go for a run, play piano, or journal, staying in the present moment and enjoying the feeling of living.
These moments of intentional presence have calmed my mind to focus in class, spend time with friends, and work on assignments. By starting off the day grounded in my body, the rest of my day unfolds with more intention, leaving me with less time on my phone and more time doing the things that have meaning in my life.
With that newfound time, I’ve been able to pursue new goals, like doing something outside of my comfort zone every day. Just yesterday, I had my first coffee chat, and, though it didn’t go as smoothly as I wanted, I learned so much about myself through reflection and writing.
Recently, I’ve also wanted to be more creative. Whether it is in writing, music, or in work, I want to embrace my creativity, challenge my perceptions of the world, and find patterns in my environment. It’s been difficult to turn off my analytical and technical lens that constantly seeks consistency, but I’ve started intentioanlly planting the buds of creativity between cracks in the concrete.
And by the quiet of night, I’ll sit back down at my desk to reflect, reviewing my intentions and goals in my journal and wrapping the day up the same way I started it: still and silent to create space to breathe and focus on living intentionally.
