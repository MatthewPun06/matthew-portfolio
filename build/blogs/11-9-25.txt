White light refracts into an infinite number of colors. It exists all around us, but the world we see isn’t made of pure white light. Instead, everything in our environment reflects only part of it back to us.
When my friend told me, “The world is a reflection of yourself,” I haven’t been able to let it escape my mind. I’ve been looking everywhere for the mirrors hidden in the world around me from the moment I wake up to the time I tuck myself in and everything in between.
If everything reflects some part of the light that touches it, then maybe the same is true for the people we meet, and those closest to us may show the clearest reflection of ourselves. My childhood friend is constantly pushing herself forward, chasing success and growth like no other. She inspires me to keep pushing forward and chasing my dreams, and I’m there to help her slow down every now and then to take care of herself. On the other hand, my mentor takes life at her own pace, so every time I talk with her, it’s as if time itself slows down and the world falls away. She reminds me that every person is incredible in their own way, and it is up to me to create a safe space to discover what makes them special. Though they don’t give me the same reflection, they show different angles and perspectives. As people become closer to me, their mirrors, initially foggy, gradually reveal a sharper reflection.
However, light doesn’t only bounce off mirrors. It bounces off everything in your environment depending on where you are, but, to see the world around you, you need to take a moment to open your eyes.
“A clean room clears your mind,” so every Tuesday, I take the time to wash my clothes and sheets, make my bed, and clean my desk. My thoughts clear as the space around me falls into place, piece by piece. By the time I am done and look at my room with its clean floors and neat stacks of books, the world slows down just enough for my shoulders to drop and my chest to loosen. My breathing becomes easier and the tension in my hands disappears. 
When watching from outside, others will never see the source of your light. As you move through your environments, the light that others see will not always be the one you put out, but they will still see the parts of you that you bring around. It’s my reminder to bring my entire self to everything I do, even when others can’t see it. If I can shine light onto everything I do, then I’ll be able to see more of the world around me and live a little brighter.
