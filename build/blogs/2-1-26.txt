The crunch of snow under your shoes as you take your first step outside into a shimmering field of freshly fallen snow is a sound I can hardly put into words. And then, after you have taken a few steps, if you look behind you, a lone trail of tiny footsteps leads towards you from the distance. I don’t think the magic of those first steps will ever fade; the idea that though hundreds of people may have walked these roads, the steps that should matter to me are the ones that I choose to take with my own two feet.
Recently, my steps have left me longing for more learning. Walking through the snow in some of my classes has been enthralling because when the snow hasn’t yet been packed, it becomes harder to slip, and I tend to take my time. So many of my classes have forced me to wade through a foot of snow, but I am gaining a deeper appreciation for how taking those first, slow steps into a unknown territory feels.
However, an issue arises when I attempt the homework, only to find that retracing those steps results in my slipping on packed snow and ice, leaving me stranded far from my comfort. The only thing to do then is to pack and melt the snow until I can see the dirt and earth beneath. Depending on the class, it might take a couple hours or a couple weeks, but it will always leave me with a road to somewhere new that I hadn’t visited before.
But who’s to say that we only walk in snow? In a class on partial differential equations (PDEs), I’ve had to retrace my steps back to multivariable calculus, packing the dirt on the way back thoroughly. As I walked backwards down the path I had frolicked with excitement down before, I saw so many forks in the road that I had missed. So many roads to explore in that forest that I couldn’t see before because I was running down the path set before me without a second thought. Though those divergences may have led even farther back, they formed connections to roads that I had walked in 7th grade, granting me a deeper appreciation and love for my ability to learn and understand.
So as you take steps onto whatever surface of your choosing — maybe it’s snow, sand, dirt, gravel, concrete, or something else entirely — take a little break to look back at how far you’ve come and then look around for any side quests that might interest you before you continue down your main path.
