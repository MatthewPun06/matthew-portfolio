Language as a form of communication is so freeing, yet so limiting in so many ways. We all carry personal definitions of every word we speak, even in different languages.
“I love you”, « Je t’aime », and “我爱你” all carry different meanings to me, though their literal translations might mean the same thing. “I love you” is so much easier for me to say, especially platonically than in any other language. It’s a way for me to show appreciation and affection for those who are close to me, but has less gravity than others might associate with it. While I rarely speak French, I (personally) would never use « Je t’aime » because, in French, I would be so imaginative and intentional about the words and phrases that I would use. My experience of the language was tinted with beauty and elegance, so, naturally, it would reflect itself in how I speak and write in the language. And finally, “我爱你” is a phrase I would only use for someone I knew I would be close with for the rest of my life, but even then, the words pale in comparison to how I would show them. Words as a medium of communication have incredibly stark limits, but it leaves space to wonder what other mediums are best suited for different situations.
In my household, most of the ways we show affection involve acts of service: cooking, cutting fruits, cleaning, driving to sports meets and concerts, etc. I have never heard the words “Love you!” uttered from any of my family members, but I have never doubted them once. Yet as I write this, I start to realize how different my definition of love is from my parents’ and, even more so, how we choose to express it still. Their love translates to their want for me to succeed, but it hides affection in layers of expectations so that even I may lose sight of it. But through their actions, it’s obvious how much they care, and it’s made me start to reciprocate so that they can understand my feelings.
Another way I have yet to remotely explore is through sounds and music. I have played for others and shown them a slice of who I am, but something I have been wanting to do is to play with someone who can play back to me. To have a conversation and harmony with another person without the use of words and purely through instruments would be an incredible experience into someone else’s identity and life while also being able to share my own at the same time. Music is also universal, crossing the barriers of language and creating understanding where language couldn’t take us, so being able to explore this side has been on my bucket list for a couple years.
The last medium of communication I wanted to explore was visual. For example. I’ve never explored the art of visual communication through paintings or dance, so I don’t know what terrain it could cover that actions, music, and language couldn’t. I’ve been trying to get into dance, but I find myself unable to express myself because I’m so focused on technique and the moves themselves, so learning how to use it as a medium rather than a result may take some time.
My ability to understand others hinges directly on the ways I can communicate with others and how they can reciprocate, so I’ll ask you—what is your favorite medium, and what can you tell me through it that no other way can quite capture it the same way?
