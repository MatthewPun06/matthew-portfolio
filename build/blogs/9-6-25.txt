This summer, I discovered Carl Jung’s theory of cognitive functions, a personality theory. While it’s not scientifically proven, it still provided valuable insights into my personality, the way to take in information, and the way I make decisions.
There are 2 types of functions: perceiving (the way you take in information) and judging (the way you make decisions). You can take in information through your senses (S) or through your intuition (N). You can make decisions using your feelings (F) or through thinking (T). Lastly, you can have a disposition to project these functions into the world around you or project them inward toward yourself, making them extraverted (e) or introverted (i) respectively.
My cognitive functions are Introverted Feeling (Fi), Extraverted Intuition (Ne), Introverted Sensing (Si), and Extraverted Thinking (Te).
My first/primary function is Introverted Feeling (Fi) which describes my resonance with rich emotions, strong values, and longing for self-reflection. It is the source of my convictions and is why I tend to spend much time alone, playing music, journaling, and running. These activities provide a space for me to perform this function.
Relying too much on introverted feeling can hinder my impact on the world around me, so my second cognitive function, Extraverted Intuition, ensures that I see the possibilities around me. It describes my ability to find patterns in the world, analyze possibilities, channel creativity, and innovate ideas. This function is closely associated with optimism for me. When I analyze future possibilities, create stories, play music, or interact with others, I try to imagine the best that could happen. When I talk to someone new, it’s usually because my optimism (Ne) inspired me to do so.
Before I was able to channel that optimism, I was subject to my third function: Introverted Sensing (Si). When combined with my first function, it created a cognitive loop because Fi and Si are both introverted functions, making it easy to get stuck inside my own head. Si helps people understand their inner selves, keeps them physically and mentally safe, and causes people to reflect on their memories. However, for me, it brings powerful feelings of nostalgia, trapping me in a longing for the past while also replacing my optimism for pessimism, keeping me from saying yes to others.
These first three cognitive functions are deeply intertwined with my feelings, so to ensure that I am not completely subject to my emotions, my fourth function, Extraverted Thinking (Te), ensures tasks are still completed on time. This function prioritizes efficiency, goal setting, statistics, and data to make decisions. While I usually wait for my optimism to fill me up with hope to make a decision, when things need to get done, Te keeps me in check. When working on a video game, I set goals and timelines to ensure I made progress. Last semester, a close friend inspired me to start journaling and setting personal/professional goals to get me to where I want to be.
Note that my first function is introverted and my second function is extraverted. According to this personality theory, all people are essentially “ambiverts” because we need ways of understanding ourselves and ways to impact the world around us. If we only live in the world outside, any change we try to enact is meaningless. If we only live in our own minds, we lose our impact on the world, so it is imperative that we foster the growth of both introversion and extraversion.
After researching this theory, I have gained much insight to my tendencies and have become more aware of how I interact with the world around me. When I am invited to new events, I remind myself to stay optimistic about how the event will go and who I will meet. When I have meaningful conversations with other people, I take the time later that day or week to reflect on what was said, changing my values, convictions, and beliefs to better reflect the information I was given. When envisioning the world in the future and the life I want, I take time to sit down, set goals, and build steps to achieve my dreams.
I hope that this inspires you to take a step back in your own life to reflect on your strengths and your potential, helping you to foster your own growth whether it is personal or professional.
