As the light refracts off the glass of street lamps and then the droplets of water on the trees surrounding them, they light up the ceiling of soft gray above my head—a backdrop to the canopies of branches without their warming coat of leaves. The sound of my rubber soles hitting the concrete slows until only the sounds of running cars can be heard. I imagine being in love, walking down the sidewalk with another. I imagine being a kid, chasing a ball and refusing to go inside. But I am standing alone, looking down the winding trail of concrete lit gently by lamps as people pass me by. Time stops continuing.
In a blink of weeks, so many wooden scraps will be thrown into a lake, but rather than let them dive deeper, I’ll stop time to hold them with my splintered hands. More will plunge in, but I’ll catch them. First 2. Then 10. Then 20. And then I stop holding. The mass continues its dive, pulling me with it. My back will slam into the scattered rocks along the bottom, digging into each crevice down my spine and across my limbs, and I’ll lie there: still.
In time, I’ll rise to the surface to find the wreckage bobbing in the waves. I might try to swim back down, reaching my hands out again. And stop—remembering how it started. Sight fogged by the blue of water, I watch. As I wait for stillness, they plunge deeper. Eventually, one by one, I guide each piece to the shore and glance down to find the reflection of grey.
I’ll tell myself to let go earlier, but sometimes my hands will forget to feel the splinters.
And, so the soft grey above now glows with light. Drops fall from the branches onto my face, pulling me into my body as I stare at the sky framed by their shape. The street lamps click off in unison as the world is illuminated. Time stops, continuing.
