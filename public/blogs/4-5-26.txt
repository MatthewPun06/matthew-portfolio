Petals, riding the wind and waltzing through the air, gently land upon the fields of green. As the scattering of pink and white bids farewell to their branches, the tree’s limbs stretch with the wind, hoping holding on for just another moment. And in their place, verdant sheets and blades emerge slowly from the branches, here to stay for the season. Yet even they will burn red and orange before falling away to the ground, leaving the branches barren once more as they reach for the ground beneath.
As I walk under the gentle light of street lamps, gentle taps soak into my shoulders and head, trickling their way down into my shoes. My friend and I, strolling under grey clouds, found ourselves at an intersection as we talked. We stood in the rain—letting the water soak into our scalps and shoes, savoring the feeling of the streams running down our face, and engraving the memory into our minds as we danced.
The morning after, we made space in her bucket for the random items she has yet to collect. We reserved a space for hiking sticks, climbing shoes, picnic blankets, and watercolor brushes, planning for the coming weeks until the end of rain. As we take the days as they come, we’ll fill the bucket of these items, and, one by one, more leaves will shine an ever so brilliant red and orange. And, by the clearing of rain, the empty space will leave the bucket and the leaves will sleep peacefully on the ground.
Like branches reaching for their petals, recent moments have stretched to fill the space, but never enough to beat the breezes sent by time. Perched on a branch, I’ll sit and watch these petals fly away, frolicking in the wind, wishing I could hold their color for another fleeting moment. And when they inevitably slip from my grasp, the canopies of green will cover my solace until even they eventually burn away in a fleeting flash of brilliance.
